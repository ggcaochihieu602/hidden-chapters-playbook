# Script Ideal — reusable remote-community documentary rules

Use this file for future “How People Live…” documentary ideas.

1. Start from the approved Alaska structure: geographic constraint → practical daily mechanism → work/economy and trade-offs → community and belonging → a midpoint turn → an ending image that returns to the opening question.
2. Keep the voice dramatic, sensory and curious, but separate verified fact, reported history, oral tradition and proposed scenes. Never invent interviews, people, quotes, population, prices, schedules or footage.
3. For each location provide: Vietnamese title; one main English title and two alternates; one-sentence theme; a 2–3 sentence hook; a 250–350 word causal summary in 5–7 beats; midpoint turn; closing image; inherited motif/new engine; verified sources; unverified claims; footage risks.
4. New versions must not repeat locations already approved in this project. Explain the story engine that makes each version distinct.
5. Prefer places with real residents and enough everyday mechanisms for a 25–35 minute documentary: access, food/water/energy, shelter, work, services, culture and future choices. A dramatic landscape alone is not enough.
6. Research primary or official sources first. Treat stock-search results as candidates, never proof of footage availability. Do a separate footage audit before promising a 30-minute edit.
7. Do not reduce Indigenous communities or living residents to exotic scenery, disaster, poverty or historical spectacle. Keep present-day agency and different personal choices visible.
8. End with a compact comparison of motif fit, novelty, visual potential and risk, then recommend one version with reasons.

Default language: English narration/title, Vietnamese editorial explanation unless the user overrides it. Do not start VO, sourcing, purchase or editing until the user approves a direction.
